### Correction DIF01

Using the product rule where $u(x)=x$ and $v(x)=\ln(x)$:
$$f'(x) = (1) \cdot \ln(x) + x \cdot \left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = \ln(x) + 1$$

Find where the derivative vanishes:
$$f'(x) = 0 \iff \ln(x) + 1 = 0 \iff \ln(x) = -1 \iff x = e^{-1} = \frac{1}{e}$$

Sign mapping:
* For $x \in ]0, e^{-1}[$, $f'(x) < 0 \implies f$ is strictly decreasing.
* For $x \in ]e^{-1}, +\infty[$, $f'(x) > 0 \implies f$ is strictly increasing.
The absolute structural minimum is locked at $f(e^{-1}) = e^{-1}\ln(e^{-1}) = -\frac{1}{e}$.
