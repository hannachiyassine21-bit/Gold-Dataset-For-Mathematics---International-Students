### Correction DE07

Rewrite the derivative using Leibniz notation, $\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x y^2$. Assuming $y \neq 0$, separate the variables by moving all $y$ terms to the left and all $x$ terms to the right:
$$\frac{1}{y^2} dy = 2x dx$$

Integrate both sides of the equation:
$$\int \frac{1}{y^2} dy = \int 2x dx \implies -\frac{1}{y} = x^2 + C \quad (\text{where } C \in \mathbb{R})$$

Invert the equation to explicitly isolate the solution $y(x)$:
$$y(x) = \frac{-1}{x^2 + C}$$

*(Note: $y(x) = 0$ is also a constant equilibrium solution to this equation).*
