SEQ01Easy
📌 Principle of Mathematical Induction
💡 Tip: Don't try to calculate u_n explicitly; trust the algebraic boundaries of the induction step.
Exercise SEQ01 (Easy)

Let \((u_n)\) be the sequence defined by \(u_0 = 2\) and:
\[u_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}u_n + 3 \quad \text{for all } n \in \mathbb{N}\]
Question: Show by induction that \(u_n < 6\) for all \(n \in \mathbb{N}\).
Correction SEQ01

Initialization: For \(n = 0\), \(u_0 = 2\), and \(2 < 6\). The property is true for \(n = 0\).

Heredity: Assume that \(u_n < 6\) for a given \(n \in \mathbb{N}\). We want to prove \(u_{n+1} < 6\).
\[u_n < 6 \implies \frac{1}{2}u_n < 3 \implies \frac{1}{2}u_n + 3 < 3 + 3 \implies u_{n+1} < 6\]
Conclusion: By the principle of induction, \(u_n < 6\) for all \(n \in \mathbb{N}\).
SEQ02Intermediate
📌 Monotonic Sequence Theorem
💡 Tip: To study the monotonicity of a fraction like this evaluate the sign of \(V_{n+1} - V_n\).
Exercise SEQ02 (Intermediate)

Consider the sequence \((v_n)\) defined for \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) by:
\[v_n = \frac{3n - 1}{n + 2}\]
Question: Prove that \((v_n)\) is strictly increasing and bounded. Deduce its convergence limit.
Correction SEQ02

\[v_{n+1} - v_n = \frac{3(n+1)-1}{(n+1)+2} - \frac{3n-1}{n+2} = \frac{7}{(n+3)(n+2)} > 0\]
So \((v_n)\) is strictly increasing. As \(n \to \infty\), \(v_n \to 3\). Bounded above by 3, below by \(v_0 = -1/2\). Converges to 3.
